# Dawn: four changes, as lines

Todd, November 5, 8:46 AM: "do all 4." Every line below is a proposal. Nothing is in the doc, the chapters, the folder or Vellum yet.

What I found first: Cal's calls already end on something he has to do himself. Ch 4 ends “Go in when she gets there.” Ch 30 ends “Go play your mother's message, Cal.” Ch 32 ends “Drive your daughter, Cal.” So the coach's assignment is already her habit. Change 1 only has to make the contrast with Janice visible.

## 1. Janice sends things. Dawn won't. (ch 4)

Where: ch 4, Dawn's call, after "I did not see how that followed. I filed it."

Now:

> “Your file stays open,” she said. “Go in when she gets there.”

Proposed:

> “What should I send her?” I said.
>
> “Nothing.”
>
> “Janice could find something.”
>
> “Janice sends things,” Dawn said. “I don't. Your file stays open. Go in when she gets there.”

Why: it's the reader's first call between the two of them, and it says which one is which in Dawn's own words. Asking what to send is Cal's habit (ch 20: “Every time, you asked me what to send.”), and Janice chose the sweet peas (ch 18), so it fits what we already know.

## 2. Take Dawn out of Janice's register (ch 4, ch 20)

Janice's sound is the short verdict ("Good for her."). Dawn's should be the question, the word handed back with a period on it, and permission. Two places where Dawn talks like Janice:

**Ch 4.** Now:

> “I have not been inside her house since. With Jo I went three hours without thinking about my mother.”
>
> “That isn't a confession, Cal. That's good news.”

Proposed:

> “I have not been inside her house since. With Jo I went three hours without thinking about my mother.”
>
> “Is that why you left out the laughing?”
>
> “I did not think I was allowed.”
>
> “You're allowed, Cal.”

Why: it removes a "That isn't X. That's Y." line. Her question makes him connect the two things himself, and it plants her word "allowed," which she gives Cal in ch 20 and Jo in ch 31.

**Ch 20.** Now:

> “What did you tell her?”
>
> “That I did not know.”
>
> “Good answer.”
>
> “It is not an answer.”
>
> “From you it is.”

Proposed:

> “What did you tell her?”
>
> “That I did not know.”
>
> “What did she say to that?”
>
> “Figure it out.”

> “And you called me.”

Why: "Good answer" and "From you it is" are Janice's sound. "And you called me." is Dawn's: his own act handed back to him, with a period on it. Denise told him to figure it out, and he called someone. The next line, his question about why she calls the women, is him starting to figure it out.

- the drive, the map shop, the stairs and the room (new)
- the dish from her mother's garage on the shelf (new; it pays off ch 4 and the "ten minutes in my truck" line later in this scene)
- the sounds over the phone become things Jo sees: the pours, the pen, the folder from ch 32
- the man outside the window carrying a framed map flat, which replaces Manny with the wardrobe boxes
- "You called me." becomes "You came to me."
- Dawn's advice, in the same words she gave Cal in ch 20, and Jo's answer to it (new)
- the ending: Jo lets herself out and hears her pour from the stairs (new)

## 4. Dawn closes the file, and Jo asks (epilogue)

Where: the epilogue, after "He asked what was for dinner." and before "On the eighth of October, a year to the day...". It's the night of the seventh: five months, which none of the eleven reached.

Proposed:

> At nine I was out on the step with the recycling when Dawn Pruitt called.
>
> “Joanna,” she said. “Five months.”
>
> “He came at ten to seven.”
>
> I heard her pour.
>
> “I closed his file in August,” she said. “I've never had one come back out of the drawer. I'll ask you the one thing.”
>
> “Let me ask you one first,” I said. “What do you want?”
>
> She didn't pour. I could hear my street.
>
> “Ask me in a month,” she said.
>
> She'd told me about the dishwasher.
>
> “I'm asking now.”
>
> “My trainer's name is Danny,” she said, and hung up.
>
> I wrote it on an envelope out of the recycling. Nobody else was going to.

Why:
- Her want in this book is for someone to ask her what she wants, and nobody has in ten years. Jo is the one who asks.
- "Ask me in a month" is Dawn's belief at work: wait until it's in order. That's what cost her the marriage, and Jo knows it.
- "I'm asking now" is what Cal said to Dawn in ch 20.
- Danny isn't named anywhere in the book yet, so his name lands here as the hook for book two, and the reader can match "trainer" to the voice in ch 32.
- Jo writing it down turns Dawn's habit around: Dawn writes down the women's first words, and Jo writes down hers. "Nobody else was going to" answers ch 20's “Because nobody else does.” If the last line reads as one beat too many, end on the envelope.
