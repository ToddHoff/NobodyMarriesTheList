## The patterns
1. **The finished speech.** Four to six sentences, no interruption, laying out a history or a pattern (Jo's list to Vili in 7, Cal's counselor story in 27, Denise's phone call in 28, Gil in 19). Fix: break it with the listener's questions.
2. **The theory of a type.** "The men who sent flowers," "husbands sent flowers to the courthouse," "half of them a mother paid for," "She tells me in four words or fewer." Fix: one person and the one thing they said.
3. **The as-you-know recap.** A character tells the other what both already know (Cal's "She told me on the first night," Jo's "You read my statement upside down. At my table in July...").
4. **The moral said out loud,** usually as a closing line or balanced contrast ("That's not the same as never going in." "I send things. I like it when they are used." "You get less. What you get tastes like something." "Nobody stays for a present").
5. **Dawn as narrator.** Her worst lines say what Jo or Cal is ("She says no fast when she's afraid of what she'd say if she waited").
6. **Recap paragraphs** in the narrator's head at chapter openings and after beats (11 to 16, 28 to 30), and pairs of closers that say the same thing twice.
7. **Repeats across chapters:** the ownership rule spoken three times in 2 and 3; the counselor story told in 27, 30 and 31; "whole weight" in 27 and 29; "every answer started with thank you" in 29 and 31.
