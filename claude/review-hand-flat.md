This is every other one in the book: 52 of "hand flat" or "hands flat" and 10 related forms ("palm flat", "flat hand", "went flat"), 61 items because two share a line.

Claude's picks: 49 plain cuts (the word "flat" comes out and nothing else changes), 9 rewordings where a plain cut loses something or repeats a phrase, and 3 to keep because the flat hand is the literal point.

## Rewordings

- **11** (ch 17): "You put your hand flat and leave it." becomes "You lay your hand on it and leave it." (Mrs. Hollis's rule for the loving tree; the character sheet has it as "Lay your hand on the old oak")
- **21** (ch 20): "I put my hand flat on the table and kept it there." becomes "I put my whole hand down on the table and kept it there." (he has been tapping with one finger, so the whole hand is the point)
- **22** (ch 20): "I kept my hand flat until the quarter was over." becomes "I kept my hand down until the quarter was over." (goes with 21)
- **24** (ch 20): "Jo put her flat hand out at him." becomes "Jo held her palm out at him." (related form; the stop signal to Pancake before "Stay.")
- **29** (ch 26): "Jo put her hand flat on one as she went by." becomes "Jo touched one as she went by." (the next sentence is "Then she stopped and put her hand on the next one", so a plain cut would say "put her hand on" twice)
- **32** (ch 27): "He put his hands flat on the seat of the chair" becomes "He put both hands on the seat of the chair" (Frank lifting himself for the cushion)
- **34** (ch 27): "put his hand flat on the back seat and then on the dash" becomes "felt the back seat and then the dash" (he is checking how hot the car is for Pancake)
- **35** (ch 27): "His hand went flat on his knee and stayed there." becomes "His hand went to his knee and stayed there." (related form; the man with the phone at the fire pit)
- **40** (ch 28): "with my hands flat on it until 10:06" becomes "with both hands on it until 10:06"

## Keep

- **33** (ch 27): "Then I held a treat out on my flat hand." (related form; this is how you feed a dog you are afraid of, and she was bitten)

- **54** (ch 35): "gave it to Pancake off my flat hand." (related form; same reason as 33)

## What the cuts leave

Taking out "flat" removes the tic. The gesture under it is still frequent: a hand on his chest ten times (4, 6, 7, 16, 17, 31, 37, 41, 57, 58), a hand on a back seven times (2, 9, 20, 23, 26, 27, 36), a hand on top of a head seven times (1, 5, 8, 15, 25, 43, 61), and Cal holding a hand on a table, desk or counter to keep still six times (21, 38, 40, 45, 55, 60). The chest, the back at the rim and the head at the doorframe are planted and paid off on purpose. The six of Cal at a table are the ones to look at if the gesture still reads as repeated after this pass.
