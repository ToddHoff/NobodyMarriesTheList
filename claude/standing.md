## How Todd wants the work done

- Before drafting: read the plan entry, the style guide, the character sheet and the two chapters before. Use the fiction-craft skill.
- No subagents on this book, for drafting, review or sweeps (October 20). The reason: a reviewer given the rules in a brief missed what Todd caught in seconds. Every check is done in the main session with the guide, the sheet and the chapters open.
- When Todd asks about a line, give options and a pick. Change nothing until he chooses (October 21). Show only the replacement line, not the line before it.
- His own line edits ("cut", a word swap): apply them and quote the new line.
- Outside critiques: give an opinion first. Change nothing until he says so.
- When he asks a question, answer it before changing anything.
- An offer he declines is closed. Do not offer it again.
- Flag anything added that he did not ask for.
- Research anything outside common knowledge before offering it, and say what was checked.
- Write proposals to a file before asking, so they outlive the session.
- Keep him posted during long work.

## The writing

The style guide carries the rules. These came later and are not in it yet:

- "Reveal character" (November 10): look first for an exchange or an action where two beliefs meet, with something at stake that nobody says, and a payoff later if possible. One invented line that only sounds like the character does not meet it.
- Every line shows character and moves the story (November 4).
- A bare "Yes" or "No" as a whole reply is a place to reveal character. Check new passages for them, and for filler.
- No line of dialogue opens with a logical "Then" (October 21).
- Jo never brings up his money to him (October 22).
- Nobody does a thing "standing up" as a detail (November 11).
- "Flat" and "hand flat" are overused (November 8 and 10). Do not add more.
- Do not give a character a new behavior for one line unless the book shows it again (November 11).
- A minor character's act comes from her trade.
- For a line meant to hit: get to the turn fast and let the reader do the arithmetic. No joke before the blow.
- When Todd asks for depth, find the fear in what the character has already lived on the page, and give one version.
- Every line comes from the character. Before proposing any line or exchange, read that person's entry in `claude/character-sheet.md` (core, the lie, how they sound) and the earlier scene the line answers. Do this first, not after a draft is rejected. A line that sums up, builds a contrast or lands on a closing line is not Jo (Todd, November 12: "Everything has to be based off her character").
- A reveal is not a flat fact (Todd, November 13: "I'm not sure why it always has to be a fact. That's not showing anything about the character and it's boring."). The sheet's voice notes (short statements, numbers, facts said flat) say how she talks, not what the line carries. A "reveal character" line shows the want or the fear she will not say out loud. A fact the reader already has shows nothing.
- A feeling can be told plainly (Todd, November 13: "We don't always need show not tell. Maybe: That gave me hope"). Offer the plain statement as an option before reaching for a gesture or a device.
- One question per reply, with numbered choices (Todd, November 13: "don't offer two suggestion where the anwer is yes. I need to be able to indicate which choice"). Never ask two things that both take "yes". If there are two matters, number every choice across both, or hold the second until the first is answered.
- "Quiet", "quietly", "stopped talking" and "in the file" are overused in this book (Todd, November 13). Do not add more.
- When a line refers to Pancake, it says Pancake.
- ", and" joins: the style guide's pre-filing check has the rule (reworded November 12).
