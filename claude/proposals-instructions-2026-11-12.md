# Proposals: instruction wording, November 12, 2026

Status, November 12, 8:28 AM: items 1 to 5 accepted by Todd and applied. Item 6 is still a question. Each item quotes what a file says now and what it would say. The reason for all of them is the same: the current models follow an instruction to the letter, so a rule worded more broadly than Todd meant gets applied more broadly than he meant.

## 1. The ", and" rule (romance-style-guide.md, "The check is Claude's own", item 1)

Now: "every ", and" join split into two sentences"

The guide was not updated after Todd's notes of October 23 ("you are just splitting the sentences. I'd like to see more variety"; "Don't be boring"). Proposed, in his terms from that day:

"every ", and" join that tacks on a second clause to land a point is resolved, and not always by a split: a subordinate clause, one sentence with one verb, cutting a half, reordering so the point lands last, or a split when that is best. No runs of short split sentences. These stay: lists, one subject's run of actions, dialogue tags, natural speech, cards, texts and screens, and callbacks repeated word for word."

## 2. Per-chapter counts (romance-style-guide.md)

Each of these is a count a literal reader will fill whether the chapter needs it or not. Proposed: keep the purpose, drop the count. Todd's call on each.

| Now | Proposed |
| --- | --- |
| "Attraction is said outright, in the narrator's body, in every chapter: the stomach, the face, the hands." | "The narrator says the attraction outright where it is live in the scene, through what they notice about the other person (October 22). Readers on this shelf do not infer it." |
| "Each Jo chapter gets 200 to 300 words of her working by herself in the house" | "Where a Jo chapter is set on a job, she has a stretch alone with the work before he arrives, so his arrival is an interruption." |
| "One act in each Jo chapter through 17 that she would call stupid in anyone else." | "Through 17, Jo is sometimes a fool for him. She is not right about everything." |
| "In each Cal chapter he reads another person fast and correctly at least once" | "Cal reads other people fast and correctly. He is wrong only about himself and about what a gift will mean." |
| "a run of "the way X does Y" similes (two or three a chapter at most)" | "the "the way X does Y" simile as a habit; keep one only where it is the best line available." |
| "One capitalized word ... about one line in four of hers at most" | "Denise's capitals are rare, and only where the stress is the joke." |

The first row matters most. The October 22 entry ("attraction shown by what they notice; the stomach lines replaced") already moved away from the body list, and the guide still names it.

## 3. Reprimands kept as rules (romance-style-guide.md and the handoff)

Lines such as "That was lazy", "It seems like you are just doing whatever you want" and "Please add depth to the story without making excuses for why you can't" record a bad day, not a rule. Proposed: keep each rule and its reason, keep Todd's words where they define the rule ("People don't speak that way"), and cut the ones that only scold.
