# Attraction details: what each one can't stop noticing

Agreed with Todd, October 22, 9:12 AM. The rule (from his reading): physical attraction is shown through specific, involuntary attention to this one person's body, often to features that aren't conventionally attractive. The test: swap in another attractive person, and the attraction should not survive. Show what the narrator can't stop noticing, not their own blush or stomach.

Use these when a romantic beat needs carrying. Prefer them to body reactions (stomach, ears hot, face warm). Let each one come back and change as they get closer.

## What Cal can't stop noticing: her hands

Todd: "her hands. They're strong, sure, and competent, and always willing to help."

1. **The callus.** Rough across the middle of her left palm, from the tape gun. Already: he feels it when she bandages his cut (4), remembers it in meetings (6), on his ribs (18), palm up while she sleeps in the truck (22). The anchor.
2. **They help before she's asked.** Half her lunch held out, the napkin across the bar, the bowl for the dog, the hutch top lifted off, tape torn with her teeth, a cord unclipped with her thumbnail (34). His mother waited to be asked and then didn't say; Jo's hands are already moving. Underused: Cal rarely says this is what gets to him.
3. **The pencil.** The carpenter's pencil behind her ear, the gray smear along the side of her hand from writing (26), her square capitals. Her hand is how the list gets struck.
4. **The one time they stop.** In 32 she pulls the fleece sleeves down over her hands; he notices because they have never hidden before. The clearest sign she is hurt.

Also on the page: the gray over her left ear she has done nothing about; the sun lines at the corners of her eyes ("on a billboard we take those out first"); the laugh that goes on in her shoulders after the sound stops.

## What Jo can't stop noticing: he owns himself

Todd: "how he moves through the world. Not like he owns it but that he owns himself."

1. **He stands.** When she walks in, every time ("He always does," 13); first for "a woman in work boots" (1). Not a show; he does it with nobody watching.
2. **He's still.** Lets the phone ring, doesn't fill a silence, "reads with his whole face still" (25), goes still in bed and looks at her (21). People moving out of a house are never still; she notices it as a mover first.
3. **The squaring.** One finger squares the card, the glass, the box to the edge. His control in one habit, and the one thing about him she teases.
4. **The parts he doesn't own.** His ears go red when he can't help it; the front tooth that sits a little behind its neighbor (the billboard leaves it out); the doorway of his mother's house he can't walk through. The cracks that make the rest believable; the ears are where she sees what he feels before he says it. Underused: the tooth appears only in 1 and should come back.

Also on the page: the gray coming in over his ears; the cuffs turned back to the elbow (4); the forty gray shirts.
