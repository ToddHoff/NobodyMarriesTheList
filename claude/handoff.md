## How Todd wants the work done

- The genre's style governs, not his personal style. It must read like a romance novel at all times.
- Sentence rules still apply: no em dashes, none of the listed AI-sounding phrases, short paragraphs whose first sentence carries story.
- Never use the name Priya. Avoid names AI tends to land on.
- A revised chapter replaces the old one. Never keep superseded chapters.
- Jo should be pretty. Attraction is stated outright in the narrator's body in every chapter.
- Jo is older than Cal (44 and 41). Owen being nine has to matter to the characters and the plot.
- Outside critiques: give an opinion first and change nothing until he says so.
- His own line notes: fix them, and show the changed lines.
- Todd, October 6: "Stop doing generic stuff. Everything must be from that character's perspective." Before any detail goes on the page, ask what this person would see, suspect or say, given their trade, their history and what they have actually been told. A detail any husband, any mover or any narrator could supply is wrong. Check it against the character sheet and the chapters, and if the sheet has nothing, say so to Todd; do not fill the gap with a stock detail.
- Todd, October 6, after two weak fixes: think things through. Make sure every voice matches its character and that what people say and do is authentic and motivated. The goal is the best, most interesting book, not the shortest change. Before touching a scene, read the chapters around it, check the character's belief and want (character sheet, and the chapter log's "wants" line), and run his fiction-craft skill. A patch that explains a turn is not a fix; the cause has to be in what the characters do.
- Get the information a task needs, especially when he directs the means.
- Do not explain how the AI agents work in the prose. Readers know.
- Source idea: matchmaker Emily Lyons's sketches. Use situations only, never her lines. The same goes for the other clips he sends: situations and registers, never the speakers' or commenters' wording.

## Things Todd has told us to keep out of the prose

These are in the style guide; listed here because they were the most frequent notes.

- "Plainly," "never once," and tidy paradoxes such as "a key I never gave him."
- Asides to the reader: "I want that on the record," "I'm not discussing it," "I should say."
- A narrator stopping a scene to state a general principle.
- Opening lines that foreshadow what the narrator has not learned yet.
- Explanations that slow the scene, especially of how scheduling or agents work.
- Trade jargon the reader will not know ("not-to-exceed").
- Exact figures that add nothing and invite mistakes (ages, hour totals).
- Wordplay that hides what is being referred to. Say the thing. A word used once for something the book calls by another name does the same harm ("cartons" for the gift boxes).
- From his chapter 7 notes: a turn that comes out of the blue. If a character decides something, the reader sees the decision and why it happens at that moment.
- Jokes that reach. If a line is meant to be funny, it has to be funny.
